# VideoFlow launch content pack

Status: drafts for review and recording. No outreach has been sent. The repositories are published privately; the app is a local preview, not an AWS production launch. Generation/payment/payout claims must pass their real-provider gates before public promotion.

## Positioning

Audience for the first pilot: independent creators making visual stories and short series. Viewers arrive through those creators' work. Begin with a focused catalogue rather than a broad promise to replace YouTube.

Public description:

> VideoFlow brings AI video creation and watching into one place. Arrange your story in a visual studio, publish to your profile, and discover what other creators make.

Hero:

> Turn your ideas into videos. Build your audience.
>
> Plan scenes in a visual studio, create with AI, and give your stories a home.
>
> Primary action: Join early access
> Secondary action: Watch the demo

For viewers:

> Find your next favourite creator. Explore public videos, follow the people you enjoy, and come back for their next release.

For creators:

> Give your next idea a place to grow. Arrange scenes, characters and shots on a canvas, then publish your work to a profile you can share.

## What people can do

- Viewers: watch public releases, search videos and follow creators.
- Creators: organise projects and scenes in Studio, use templates, generate through the platform's configured service, and publish with visibility/audience controls.
- Account owners: edit their profile, feature a public video, enable MFA/passkeys and manage sessions.
- Founding testers: make a first project, report friction, suggest templates and share work they want featured.

## Commercial explanation

Generation credits and viewing Premium are separate products. Credits pay for generation; the platform chooses the underlying model, so there is no model-selection menu. Premium is one platform-wide viewing subscription, not a separate subscription to each creator.

Internal business model: margin between provider cost and retail credit price, plus Premium revenue. Price credits only after measuring generation, storage, delivery, payment fees, failures/refunds and operating costs. Do not advertise an unmeasured price or guaranteed output quality/time.

Crypto payments and view-based rewards are not active in the local release. Approved future creator rewards come from a capped revenue pool with published eligibility and anti-fraud rules; views alone never imply a guaranteed payment. Do not use "earn per view", income forecasts or payout availability as launch promises.

## 40-second demo recording script

Record the actual app. Use a completed project and a real provider-produced output once generation is approved; never pass a mock render off as a real generation.

| Time | Screen | Voiceover |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–5s | Finished short video, labelled as AI-created | "A story starts with an idea." |
| 5–13s | Original Studio canvas: scenes, characters, shots | "VideoFlow gives you a visual space to arrange it." |
| 13–21s | Edit one scene; show a real generation quote | "Shape a scene and see the credit cost before you create." |
| 21–27s | Actual job progression, with time cuts labelled | "Follow the progress, then review the result." |
| 27–34s | Publish controls and public profile | "Publish to your profile and choose who can watch." |
| 34–40s | Discover and following | "Create your next story. Discover someone else's. Join VideoFlow early access." |

Until provider testing is complete, release a Studio walkthrough that ends before generation and says "Early development preview" on screen. Do not use the full script to imply an operational paid service.

## First seven social posts

1. **Introduction.** "We're building VideoFlow: a visual studio for AI video stories, with a place to publish and discover them. Looking for early creators who want to help shape the experience." Attach a real canvas walkthrough. Action: join early access.
2. **Creator workflow.** "One story, several scenes. Here's how we arrange characters, shots and references in VideoFlow." Show one focused edit. Action: suggest a workflow improvement.
3. **Viewer experience.** "You don't have to make videos to enjoy VideoFlow. Discover public releases and follow creators you want to watch again." Show actual browsing. Action: tell us what you want to watch.
4. **Profile reveal.** "Your work deserves a home. Add your bio and links, then feature a public video on your VideoFlow profile." Show an approved creator profile. Action: share what belongs on a creator page.
5. **Tutorial.** "Your first VideoFlow project: start a project, arrange a scene, and add your characters." Keep one task per tutorial. Action: try it and report where you get stuck.
6. **Creator spotlight.** Publish only with permission: the creator's own explanation, real output and actual process. Avoid invented testimonials. Action: watch their work and follow them.
7. **Pilot invitation.** "We're inviting a small group of founding creators to test VideoFlow with us. Bring a story idea, publish when you're ready, and help us improve the tools." Action: join the pilot.

Always caption demos. Cross-posting realistic synthetic media needs the destination platform's AI disclosure; see YouTube's altered/synthetic-content guidance and TikTok's AI-generated-content guidance below. Use music/visuals the publisher has rights to distribute.

## Outreach drafts — do not send automatically

Creator invitation:

> Hi — I enjoyed your work on [specific project]. We're building VideoFlow, a visual studio and publishing space for AI video stories. We're looking for a small founding group to test the workflow and tell us what feels missing. Would you be interested in a short walkthrough? There's no promise of audience or earnings; we'd value your feedback.

Personalise the bracketed project reference before sending. No bulk messages, scraped lists or fabricated familiarity.

Community organiser:

> We're building VideoFlow for people making visual stories with AI. Would your community be interested in a small, optional creation session or themed showcase? We can provide a product walkthrough and collect feedback. We'd agree the format and any credit budget with you first.

## First-month execution checklist

### Week 1 — show the product truthfully

- [ ] Confirm public preview access, support contact, privacy/terms and launch owner.
- [ ] Record the development walkthrough with captions and approved footage.
- [ ] Prepare five real example works with their creators' permission and AI disclosures.
- [ ] Publish the hero, viewer/creator explanation and three short tutorials.
- [ ] Validate real provider costs and failures before publishing credit prices.
- [ ] Publish payment/refund and content-rights explanations before enabling checkout.

### Week 2 — help creators succeed

- [ ] Personally invite 10–20 relevant creators, with community permission where required.
- [ ] Help each creator finish one project; document the top points of friction.
- [ ] Ask permission for spotlights and collect specific, unedited feedback.
- [ ] Keep any pilot credit grants capped, audited and separate from cash earnings.

### Week 3 — bring viewers to actual work

- [ ] Share three approved creator spotlights and two focused tutorials.
- [ ] Link clips to the full release/profile, not an empty homepage.
- [ ] Check mobile watching, profile sharing and follow flows.
- [ ] Track first publications, returning viewers and repeat creators.

### Week 4 — learn before paid acquisition

- [ ] Run a small themed challenge only after agreeing rules, rights and any costs.
- [ ] Publish selected work with creator permission.
- [ ] Review failures, cost per successful video and support burden.
- [ ] Decide whether retention and contribution margin support a wider launch.

## Success measurements

Use account events rather than inflated reach counts. Record only consented, necessary analytics and exclude staff/test accounts.

- Creator activation: first published video / registered creators who attempted creation.
- Creation completion: successful published outputs / started generation jobs; report technical failures separately from user abandonment.
- Repeat creation: creators publishing in two separate weeks / activated creators.
- Viewer return: viewers watching again on a later day / first-time viewers.
- Paid conversion: first credit purchases / eligible activated creators, once checkout is live.
- Contribution margin: collected revenue minus provider, processing, delivery/storage, refunds and allocated creator-reward costs. Include failed-job costs.

Treat pilot results as learning, not evidence of market-wide demand. Set numeric acquisition/retention targets after observing the pilot baseline.
